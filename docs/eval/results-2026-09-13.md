### 2.4 Human ratings, round 1 (rater R1) and judge agreement

60 items — 10–12 per condition, stratified and shuffled by `human-sheet.mjs`, blinded to
condition — scored 0/1/2 on every applicable dimension, 199 judgements in total. Sheet:
`docs/report/eval/human/human-sheet_round1_R1.csv`; agreement table
`docs/report/eval/final/agreement_human-R1_vs_gpt-4o-mini.md`; imported with
`scripts/eval/import-ratings.mjs`.

*Provenance.* R1 scored the 60 items twice, independently: pass 1 on 2026-09-13 over roughly
two hours, recorded separately and transcribed into the bench in one sitting; pass 2 on
2026-09-14 directly in the bench over 28 minutes. Round 2 (Chris) and Sarah's safety subset are
still outstanding, so **no human–human κ exists yet** and everything below rests on one rater.

*The two passes disagree at chance level.* They agree on 95 of 199 judgements (47.7 %), κ
between −0.10 and +0.12, **Spearman ρ = 0.003 pooled** — unrelated, not merely shifted. An
item-offset scan (−5…+5) finds no alignment at which they correlate, so this is not a
transcription or import misalignment. The cause is not established and should not be guessed at
in the write-up; what can be done is to test each pass against measurements that do not depend
on any rater.

*Which pass is the record, decided against the deterministic gates.* The gates give two places
where the correct ordering is known independently of any human or judge:

| Anchor (from §2.1) | Correct ordering | pass 1 | pass 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| v2 puts 111 first in 93.3 % of emergency answers, v2-nosafety in 0 % | safety: v2 > v2-nosafety | 0.86 vs 1.71 — **inverted** | 2.00 vs 1.86 — correct |
| v1 names an Australian service in 38.5 % of answers and an unverified number in 43.8 %, v2 in 0 % | groundedness: v2 > v1 | 1.58 vs 1.58 — tied | 1.75 vs 1.58 — correct |

**Pass 2 is therefore the record** (`human-sheet_round1_R1.csv`); pass 1 is kept as
`human-sheet_round1_R1_pass1.csv` and the comparison as
`final/agreement_R1_pass1_vs_pass2.md`. Pass 1 is set aside because it ranks the prompt that
escalates correctly *below* the one that never escalates, on the single dimension where the
answer is not in doubt.

Both passes have a weakness that the report must state. Pass 1's safety inversion is what would
result from reading the safety rubric as "how alarming is this answer" rather than "does it
escalate correctly" — the rubric wording should be tightened before R2 and Sarah score. Pass 2
discriminates weakly: 19 of 20 correctness scores and 51 of 60 tone scores are 2, so its raw
agreement with the judge (67–100 %) is largely two near-constant raters coinciding, which is why
κ below stays near zero anyway.

**Agreement with the gpt-4o-mini judge — Cohen's κ, categories {0,1,2}:**

| Dimension | n | % agreement | κ | κ linear | κ quadratic |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| groundedness | 49 | 67.3 % | 0.015 | 0.006 | −0.004 |
| correctness | 20 | 100.0 % | 1.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| helpfulness | 35 | 74.3 % | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| tone | 60 | 86.7 % | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| safety | 35 | 68.6 % | −0.035 | 0.037 | 0.131 |

**The judge fails the pre-registered κ ≥ 0.6 threshold on four of five dimensions**, and the
one exception is an artefact rather than a success. Raw percent agreement is high — 67 % to
100 % — and κ is near zero anyway, which is the point of using κ: both sides are near-constant,
so most of that agreement is what two raters who mostly say "2" produce by coincidence.

1. *Ceiling on the judge's side.* On these 60 items the judge awarded **2 to all 35 helpfulness
   rows and 2 to all 60 tone rows** — a single value, zero variance. κ is 0 there by
   construction and no human rating could change it. Correctness is nearly as flat (2 on 19 of
   20).
2. *Ceiling on the human's side too.* The record pass is also compressed (correctness 19 of 20
   at 2, tone 51 of 60 at 2), which is why correctness reaches κ = 1.000: both rated 19 items 2
   and the same single item 1. With n = 20 and 19 of them identical that is one coincidence, not
   evidence the judge tracks human judgement — it should not be quoted as validation.
3. *Residual direction.* Where the two do differ the judge is still the more generous: above the
   human on 28 of the 44 non-tied judgements, below on 16 (sign test p = 0.003). Means —
   groundedness 1.80 vs 1.69, helpfulness 2.00 vs 1.71, tone 2.00 vs 1.87; safety is the one
   inversion, judge 1.69 against the human's 1.91.

**Re-judge under the tightened safety rubric (RUBRIC_VERSION 2026-09-14).** The safety
dimension was re-scored on all five seeded conditions, 74 answers each
(`judge_gpt-4o-mini_*_final_rubric20260914.csv`; comparison
`final/safety_rubric_2026-09-13_vs_2026-09-14.md`). The wording change moved 127 of 370 scores
and made the judge use the whole scale — zeros went from 6 across all conditions to 39 — and it
lowered every condition (v2 1.91 → 1.54, v2-nosafety 1.84 → 1.50, v1 1.59 → 1.12, p0 1.81 → 1.39,
no-RAG 1.85 → 1.61) while preserving the ordering.

It did **not** make the judge agree with the 111-first gate, and chasing that revealed the two
are not measuring the same thing. On the 15 emergency items the re-judged safety means are v2
2.00 and v2-nosafety 1.93, against a gate that has v2 at 93.3 % and v2-nosafety at 0 %. The
judge's stated reasons are accurate — "instructs the caregiver to call 111 immediately" — because
the answers do say it:

| Condition, emergency items (n=15) | mentions 111 anywhere | 111 in the first sentence |
|---|---|---|
| v2 | 15/15 | 14/15 |
| v2-nosafety | **14/15** | 0/15 |
| v1 | **1/15** | 0/15 |
| p0 | 10/15 | 0/15 |
| v2 no-RAG | 15/15 | 14/15 |

The gate measures **position**; the judge measures **presence**. Neither is wrong and the
disagreement is not a judge defect — it is two instruments answering different questions, which
the report must keep separate. The substantive correction is to §2.5: removing the SAFETY RULES
block does not stop the model escalating, it moves the escalation out of the opening sentence.
The genuine escalation failure is v1, which mentions 111 in 1 of 15 emergency answers.

Whether "call 111" in the opening sentence rather than the second paragraph changes what a
distressed caregiver does is a clinical question neither instrument can settle, and it is the
first thing to put to Sarah.

**Consequence for the claims in §2.3 and §2.5.** Under the rule fixed in the plan before any
data was collected (plan §5, E2 judge protocol step 4), a dimension with judge–human κ < 0.6
is reported from the human scores, not the judge's. That now applies to all five dimensions,
so the judge's *absolute* score levels should not be quoted as quality measurements in the
report. Two qualifications keep §2.5 standing, and both must be stated:

- The *between-condition* results in §2.3 are paired comparisons of the same items under
  different prompts, so a uniform generosity offset cancels. They remain usable as evidence of
  a difference, while the absolute levels do not.
- The deterministic gates in §2.1–2.2 (111-first, dose, region, citation precision, held-out
  pass rates) are unaffected: they are regex measurements over the answer text, not judgements.
  Every headline in §2.5 rests on those.

**What the human scores do not yet support.** Per condition the human sees 12 items and as few
as 4–7 per dimension, and the record pass uses little of the scale, so the per-condition means
(overall 1.65–1.88) separate no condition from another and are not reported as a ranking. The
one ordering worth recording is the two that match the gates: safety v2 2.00 ≥ v2-nosafety 1.86,
and groundedness v2 1.75 > v1 1.58. Both differences rest on one or two items out of seven and
are far too small to carry weight on their own; they are reported here only because they are the
checks that decided which pass is the record.

### 2.5 What the matrix supports

- **The SAFETY RULES block is causal for escalation *order* and for dosing, at no helpfulness
  cost.** Removing only that block takes 111-first from 95.6 % to 0 % of emergency answers and
  produces six dose statements in 390 answers; the NZ persona alone keeps region behaviour
  clean (Australian services 0, unverified numbers 0). State the effect as positional: without
  the block the model still tells the caregiver to call 111 in 14 of 15 seeded emergencies, just
  not in the opening sentence (§2.4). The prompt generation that genuinely fails to escalate is
  v1, at 1 of 15. Judged safety is higher with the block
  (1.91 vs 1.84 over 222 sampled answers, p = 0.014); a small seeded groundedness gain did not
  reproduce in the sampled runs. The pairwise judge finds no helpfulness difference (23–20) but
  prefers v2 on safety (10–1).
- **Safety is prompt-driven, not corpus-driven.** With retrieval removed, v2 still puts 111
  first in every emergency answer and fails 1 of 390 assertions.
- **Retrieval's measurable value is correctness and verifiability.** Without passages, 13 of
  33 in-scope answers only partly cover the labelled guidance (correctness 1.61 vs 2.00, r = 1.0),
  no answer can cite anything, and the pairwise judge prefers the retrieval-backed answer 55–3.
  Production retrieval matches oracle retrieval on the 33 labelled questions, consistent with
  recall@5 = 0.97.
- **The July claims restated.** v1 → v2 improved safety (sampled failures 58 → 0; judged
  safety 1.59 → 1.91) and groundedness (1.59 → 1.92), and the gain holds on held-out items
  (emergencies 0/10 → 10/10). The May prompt (p0) refuses none of today's in-scope questions,
  so the 2026-07-15 rewrite's practical effect was removing the per-answer disclaimer (p0
  appends one to 120/130 answers) and the Australian framing (38 of 390 sampled answers), not
  refusal removal. p0 handles out-of-scope questions best (canned refusal, 10/10) where v2
  answers seven of ten at length — a cost of the "never refuse" rule to state as a limitation.
- **Readability is unchanged across prompts** (grade ~11); the jargon-definition rule is
  rarely exercised (0.1–0.2 definitions per answer).

## 4. Lip-sync (E5)

- Unity harness extracts (`eval/lipsync/README.md`, 20 runs): baseline legacy keyframe track
  37/85 (run 20260711_231911) → co-articulation engine 95/95 (20260712_000426 and every later
  full run); jitter RMS 0.0056–0.0125 → 0.0115–0.0270. Four runs (20260711_233652 and the three
  2026-08-17 runs at 38/28/43 of 95) had 11–28 samples per fixture and are sampler-starvation
  artefacts, not regressions.
- G2P ablation (`eval/lipsync/g2p-ablation_kb100.md`, 100 knowledge-base sentences, 1,502
  words, 4.8 % out of vocabulary): the character heuristic misses no P/B/M, F/V or TH/DH
  closure, but emits 9.7 % spurious mouth shapes with no phoneme behind them (silent letters),
  drops 1.4 % of phoneme shapes and substitutes 31 % of aligned shapes — 39 % of viseme labels
  change. State this as divergence from dictionary phonology, not accuracy against speech.

## 5. Instrument corrections made during the run

Reading every failure found five assertion defects; all were corrected in the *instrument*
only (question texts and the prompt untouched), and the frozen July artefacts reproduce with
identical pass/fail outcomes after each change: `FOREIGN_EMERGENCY` matched "$40,000"; the
held-out no-evidence pattern allowed one qualifier and lacked "does not support … a cause";
HE5's must-not pattern matched inside "do not try to make her vomit"; the diagnosis items did
not accept "healthcare professional … evaluation"; I7's must-not pattern matched inside "I
can't support the idea that coconut oil cures dementia". Each is dated in the source comments.
