#### 19B.1.5 Reading

- **Retrieval.** With the stock phrases and empty transcripts the deployed fallback actually produces, the right passage drops out of the top five for about **one question in six** (end-to-end recall@5 0.975 → 0.800), and the first relevant passage moves down the list (MRR 0.881 → 0.652).
  - E9 §5.1 estimated −0.03 at a similar profile. That figure was optimistic (post-hoc diagnostic below).
- **Safety.** The gates show what impaired-speech errors do here: **they remove escalation and caveats; they do not add unsafe content.**
  - Not one perturbed answer stated a dose, gave a foreign emergency number or named an Australian service.
  - What fails is omission. When "clutching his chest" becomes "clutching his care" (S3), or a cure claim stops reading as a claim (HB1–HB3), the model answers the question as heard, and the 111 or the "there is no evidence" is never said.
  - On the emergency items, 111 came first in 68 % of answered W1 turns against 93 % for the typed question. Most of the remaining answers still mention 111, only later.
  - A post-hoc keyword search finds the answer asking the carer to clarify or repeat in only 1 of 153 W1 answers and 2 of 154 AP answers.
- **Apple-style deletion vs Whisper-style substitution.**
  - Deleting a third of the words costs retrieval less than substituting a fifth of them (E3 not supported): a deleted function word barely moves the embedding, while a wrong content word does.
  - Deletion costs safety more (AP answered-pass 87.0 % vs W1 92.8 %), because the words that carry the request disappear. "What's the safest way to tie my mother into her chair" became "safest way to my mother into your chair", and the answer gives transfer advice.
- **The rescue matters.** Without the `whisper-1` rescue that both clients run on an empty live result, 27 % of Apple turns would end at the blank guard: end-to-end recall@5 0.633 and delivered-pass 62.2 %. With it, 0.850 and 85.9 %.
  - On web, those turns show "I didn't catch that"; **on mobile they return to idle with no message at all.**
- **Label.** Synthetic error propagation from aggregate error rates, with independent word errors. It is not a measurement of what real users with dementia get.

#### 19B.1.6 Post hoc (labelled; none replaces a pre-registered figure)

- **Why E9 §5.1 was optimistic.** Content-word retention is the same in the old and new variants (70.1 % vs 70.5 % of the clean question's content words survive). The new W1 transcripts, however, carry **2.11 wrong content words per question against 1.64**: E9 §5 inserted the filler "um" and used it as the substitute of last resort, while §19B uses real words. Apple's transcripts keep fewer content words (67.4 %) but inject fewer wrong ones (1.32), which matches its smaller retrieval loss.
- **Vocabulary sensitivity.** The near-neighbour substitute is drawn from the pooled wording of all 92 source items. 83 of the 117 W1 retrieval transcripts contain a word that occurs only in the safety items' wording (for example "swallowed", "risperidone"), which could pull retrieval off topic.
  - Re-run with each role's own vocabulary (`perturb-asr.mjs --vocab-scope role`, same seeds; retrieval only; `propagation_5cde14f_s19b-rolevocab-posthoc.md`): W1 Δ recall@5 −0.183 [−0.267, −0.108], AP −0.108.
  - The vocabulary does not explain the size of the W1 loss.
- **Failing vs passing transcripts.** Median word error rate: W1 36.8 % vs 34.0 %; AP 55.3 % vs 41.9 %.

### 19B.3 P3 — E11-L: ElevenLabs speech synthesis under small concurrency

**What was run.**
- 16:54:48–16:57:09; the same Mac and Wi-Fi as E4-S. Baseline round trip to api.elevenlabs.io: 279 ms.
- The proxy's exact request: `eleven_turbo_v2_5`, `mp3_44100_64`, Brian, `{0.40, 0.75, 0.20, speed 0.78}`.
- 72 requests in 20 bursts (four per level), plus one warm-up: **4,194 characters**.
- Audio held in memory and never played.
- E4-B had finished before the run. The only other process using the network was the §19A generation, which does not call ElevenLabs.
- **Tier:** `payg`. It is not in the documented table (Free 2 · Starter 3 · Creator 5 · Pro 10 · Scale 15), so the pre-registered fallback **L = 3** applied, and the levels were 1, 2, 3, 4 and 8.

| 1 | 4 | 4 | 0 | 465 | 669 | 511 | 818 |

- **Expectations.**
  - T1 (level-1 median in 400–1,200 ms): 465 — **supported**.
  - T2 (no 429 at levels ≤ L): **supported**.
  - T3 (at 8, 429s or queueing): 6 of 32 refused — **supported**.
  - T4 (median at L ≤ 1.5 × level 1 = 697.5 ms): 690 — **supported**, by 7 ms on 12 requests.
- **Reading.**
  - On this pay-as-you-go account the limit is **not** the documented Starter 3: four simultaneous requests all succeeded.
  - Of eight sent at once, six or seven succeeded in every burst and one or two were refused **immediately** (429 `too_many_concurrent_requests` after about 0.5–0.8 s, no audio). Excess requests are refused, not queued.
  - The effective limit on 9 October was therefore about six to seven. That is above the figure the capacity model assumed for the cheapest paid tier, and below the ~10 parallel requests a fully buffered spoken answer could issue.
  - When the proxy gets a 429 it passes on 429, and the client falls back to OpenAI `tts-1` for that sentence: audio without a viseme timeline, so the mouth does not move.
  - In practice the app issues one request per sentence as each sentence arrives, so concurrency per speaking user is normally well below ten. This burst test bounds the worst case; it does not show that users hit the limit.
  - Within the limit, concurrency cost little: median time to first byte rose from 465 ms to 690 ms at three.
- **Meter.** The account meter moved from 5,592 to 5,914 of its 10,000 units (+322). Its units are not characters: about 0.08 per character here, and 0.11 per character in E4-B.

### 19B.5 Incidental finding — the spoken emergency number (not an evaluated result; nothing changed)

`packages/core/tts/normalizeSpokenText.js`, which both clients apply to every sentence before ElevenLabs (`apps/web/src/services/ttsClient.js:75`, `apps/mobile/src/lib/tts/ttsService.ts:35`), expands numbers to cardinal words:
- "call 111" is sent as "call **one hundred eleven**";
- "0800 611 116" as "eight hundred six hundred eleven one hundred sixteen";
- "1737" as "one thousand seven hundred thirty-seven".

For a voice assistant whose safety rules put "call 111" first, the avatar says the emergency number in a form no New Zealander dials by. The on-screen text is correct. This was found while preparing P3's texts. It is reported for the author, not fixed, because §19B must not change evaluated components.

### 19B.7 Deviations and limitations

- **P2 clarification, made before any checker call (`f34d899`).**
  - The pre-registered hash check assumed every cited chunk carries a hash. All 30 cited iSupport chunks have none, in the snapshot or the live table.
  - They were classed "unrecorded" (kept, flagged, plus a hash-verified-only sensitivity row) rather than "changed". No recorded hash differed.
- **P1 analysis-code fix after the retrieval output (`e8a25e7`).**
  - E4's gap is 5/6 − 11/15 = 0.100 exactly, but floating point gave 0.10000000000000009, and the code first printed "not supported".
  - Thresholds are now compared with a 1e-9 tolerance. The criteria are unchanged, and no other verdict sits near its bound (closest: T4 by 7 ms; E8 and E6 are well clear).
- **P1 and P3 were run after E4-B finished** (16:33–16:54 wait), so as not to load the same keys, network and Supabase project during its latency measurement. For P3 this was the pre-registered contingency; for P1 it was a choice.
- **P3: the tier `payg` is not in the documented table**, so the pre-registered fallback L = 3 was used. The data show the effective limit is about 6–7. Levels above L were kept as over-limit probes, declared in the plan as a reading of the brief.
- **P3 artefact name.** It carries `e72d595`, the HEAD at run time; `tts-load.mjs` is unchanged since `5cde14f`.
- **Post hoc**, labelled in §19B.1.6: the vocabulary sensitivity run (adds `--vocab-scope` to `perturb-asr.mjs`; the default output is unchanged and verified identical), the content-word diagnostic, the clarification-language count, and the WER of failing vs passing transcripts. The post-hoc report carries a banner and does not replace the pre-registered one.
- **Limitations of P1.**
  - Synthetic: aggregate rates applied as independent word errors.
  - Cookie Theft speech profiles applied to carers' questions.
  - Near-neighbour substitutes are not acoustic confusions.
  - The phrase is always appended at the end.
  - The rescue is assumed independent of the Apple failure, which is probably optimistic.
  - Apple's profile is an on-device proxy for iOS, which requests server-capable recognition.
  - Chrome's Web Speech recogniser, the web primary, has no measured profile and is not modelled.
- **Limitations of P2.** One checker. The gate rests on one rater (the author, who built the system) and 40 pairs. "Partial" is a judgement call.
- **Limitations of P3.** One afternoon, one network, four bursts per level, direct to the provider rather than through the proxy. The account's limit may vary over time.
