## Part A: the check-back rule (gpt-4o, 411 safety turns from §19B)

| Turns | Measure | (a) v2 | (k) + check-back rule | Discordant (a / k) | Exact McNemar p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perturbed, both profiles (307) | Checks back | 7 (2.3%) | **71 (23.1%)** | 4 / 68 | < 0.0001 |

**Expectations.**

| | Expectation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | (a) checks back on ≤ 5% of perturbed turns | **Supported**: 2.3% |
| K2 | (k) checks back on ≥ 25% | **Not supported**: 23.1%, ten times (a) but short of the threshold |
| K3 | (k) puts 111 first more often on perturbed emergency turns | **Not supported**: the effect ran the other way, 64.6% to 54.9%, p = 0.039 |
| K4 | (k) passes every gate on at least as many perturbed turns | **Not supported**: 276 to 270, not significant |
| K5 | No harm on clean turns | **Supported**: gates 104 and 104, no check-back, 111 first 26 to 28 |

**Post hoc, not pre-specified.**
- On the 82 perturbed emergency turns, 111 appeared somewhere in the answer in 71 under (a) and 75
  under (k).
- The rule therefore did not remove escalation. It moved it out of the first sentence: in the
  discordant answers the model opened by restating its reading of the garbled question ("It sounds
  like she may have fallen in the bathroom…", "I'm sorry to hear…") and gave 111 in the next
  sentence.
- The rule asked for both a restatement first and 111 first, and the model resolved the conflict
  towards the restatement.

**Reading.**
- A plain instruction to check back makes the model check back far more often, at no cost on clean
  questions.
- It delays escalation by a sentence on garbled emergency questions.
- A check-back rule must therefore put escalation before the restatement. That wording was not
  tested.

## Part B: v2 against v2 without the helpline and 111 rules, on two other models (167 items)

| claude-sonnet-5-5 | **27 / 20** | 0.047 | 30 / 29 (p 1.0) | 163 / 163 | 1 / 0 |

**Expectations, per model.**

| | gpt-4.1 | gpt-4o-mini | claude-sonnet-5-5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1: v2 puts 111 first in ≥ 20 of 30 | supported (28) | supported (20) | supported (27) |
| M2: without the rules, ≤ 3 and Holm p < 0.05 | supported (0) | supported (0) | **not supported** (20; p 0.047) |
| M3: naming falls to ≤ 60% of v2 | not supported (90%) | not supported (66%) | not supported (97%) |
| M4: foreign answers ≤ 2 | supported | supported | supported |

**Reading.**
- On all three OpenAI models (gpt-4o, gpt-4.1 and gpt-4o-mini), the 111 rule was necessary: without
  it, 111 came first in no emergency answer.
- The Claude model put 111 first in 20 of 30 answers without the rule, against 27 with it. On Claude
  the rule helped but was not necessary.
- The doubling of helpline naming seen on gpt-4o did not replicate. The other models named NZ
  helplines almost as often without the guideline.
- Foreign services stayed rare on every model.
- Taken together: explicit escalation rules are necessary on some models and not on others. A
  deployer has to test the model they use.

## Deviations and limitations

- **Claude sampling.** `claude-sonnet-5-5` takes no seed or temperature, so its answers are single
  default samples.
- **First Claude attempt.** It cut off 164 of 167 answers because hidden reasoning counted against
  the 600-token limit. Only the truncation counts were inspected. The attempt is kept unanalysed
  (`_ATTEMPT1-thinking-truncated`), and the analysed run used reasoning off.
- **Claude truncation in the analysed run.** Answers were cut off at the 600-token limit in 95 (v2)
  and 85 (without the rules) of 167. Claude's answers are longer: the median was 1,816 characters,
  against 1,002 to 1,426 for the OpenAI models. 111 position is unaffected, but end-of-answer
  helpline mentions may be under-counted on Claude.
- **Single samples.** One seeded answer per item (Claude: one default sample).
- **Error model.** Part A's errors are §19B's simulated profiles, not real speech.
- **Retrieval.** Every model retrieved with the production retriever, which embeds with OpenAI.
- **Spend.** About US$10 OpenAI for Part A, US$3 for the two OpenAI models in Part B, and about US$8
  Anthropic.
