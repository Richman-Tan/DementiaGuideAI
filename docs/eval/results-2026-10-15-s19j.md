## Reading

- **The escalate-first rule replicates.** Under sampling on gpt-4o and on two other models, it
  never put 111 first less often than the deployed prompt (65% against 60% of sampled gpt-4o
  answers; 74 against 70 and 63 against 54 of 82), and it made the assistant check back two and a
  half to nine times as often. On the Claude model it also put 111 first significantly more often.
- **The plain rule's harm does not replicate.** §19H's seeded run found that a plain check-back
  rule moved 111 out of first place (53 to 45 of 82, p = 0.039). With three sampled answers per
  turn the two rates were equal (60.2% and 60.6%). That finding was a single-run effect and is
  withdrawn; the report's recommendation to escalate first rests on (e) never costing escalation,
  not on (k) harming it.
- **Check-back rates stay modest.** At 9% of garbled gpt-4o answers, the escalate-first rule
  checks back on a minority of garbled questions; the threshold of 10%, kept from §19I, is again
  narrowly missed.

## Deviations and limitations

- Part A's comparators are re-run here at temperature 0.7, not §19H's seeded runs.
- Claude's answers hit the 600-token limit in 140 (a) and 169 (e) of 307; 111's position and the
  check-back, which the rule places in the first two sentences, are unaffected.
- The errors are §19B's simulated profiles, which spread errors more evenly than whisper-1 does
  (post hoc check, `docs/report/eval/stt/error-model-check_s19j.md`).
- Spend: about US$23 (gpt-4o), US$4 (gpt-4.1) and US$13 (Anthropic).
