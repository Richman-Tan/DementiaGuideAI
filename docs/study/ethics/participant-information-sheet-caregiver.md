**Kia ora,**

My name is Richman Tan and I am a final-year engineering student at the University
of Auckland. I would like to invite you to take part in a research study. Please
read this sheet, and take as long as you need to decide. You are under no obligation
to take part.

## What is this study about?

Information about caring for someone with dementia exists, but people often tell us
it is hard to find, scattered across many places, and written in language that is
hard to use when you are tired and stressed.

We have built an app that answers questions about dementia care. It uses a library
of trusted, New Zealand-specific information, and it can answer either as **text on
the screen** or as **a talking, animated character that speaks the answer aloud**.

What we do not know is whether the talking character actually helps. That is what
this study is for: we want to find out which way of using the app works better for
the people who would actually use it.

## What would I have to do?

Everything happens online, on your own device, whenever suits you. There is no
appointment and nobody will be watching you.

1. You will get a link, a participant code and an access code by email.
2. The website will explain the study and ask for your consent.
3. You will answer a few short questions about yourself — no name, no contact
   details.
4. You will be given a short, made-up situation — for example, *someone who becomes
   restless and upset in the evenings* — and asked to use the app to find out what
   could help. You will do three of these one way, and three the other way.
5. After each way of using the app, you will answer some questions about how easy it
   was to use.
6. At the end there are five short questions about what you thought.

**It takes about 35 to 45 minutes.** You can take a break between the two halves.

You will need a device with a microphone and an internet connection, and you will
need to use Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge — the talking part of the app does not
work properly in other browsers.

## Do I have to talk about my own situation?

No. The situations we give you are made up, and you do not need to tell the app — or
us — anything about your own circumstances. **Please do not type or say real names,
addresses, or anything else that would identify you or the person you care for.**

You can skip any question. You can stop at any time by using the **"I need to stop"**
button, which is on every screen.

## What are the risks?

The subject is dementia care, which may be an emotionally difficult topic. You may
find that thinking about these situations brings up feelings about your own
experience.

If you would like to talk to someone at any point:

- **111** — emergency
- **Healthline 0800 611 116** — free 24/7 health advice from a nurse
- **Alzheimers NZ 0800 004 001** — dementia support and local services
- **1737** — free call or text any time to talk to a trained counsellor

**Please note:** the app gives general information only. It is **not** medical
advice, and it cannot diagnose anything or tell you what to do about a specific
person. Always talk to a doctor, nurse or other health professional about real
decisions. The app is a research prototype and may sometimes give answers that are
incomplete or wrong — that is part of what we are testing.

## What are the benefits?

There is no direct benefit to you, and there is no payment. Your feedback will shape
how this kind of tool is built for carers in New Zealand, and this is a real gap —
carers consistently report that the care system is complex and fragmented and that
information is hard to find.

## What information is collected, and what happens to it?

| What we collect | Why |
|---|---|
| Your answers to the questionnaires | To measure how usable each version is |
| How long each task took, and how many questions you asked | To measure how efficient each version is |
| **The questions you asked the app and the answers it gave** | The app saves your conversations so you can return to them, as any messaging app does. For the study, they also let us check whether you found the right information and whether the app ever gave unsafe advice |
| Technical information — your browser, and how fast the app responded | To understand any problems you ran into |

We do **not** collect your name, email address, IP address, or any recording of your
voice. Your spoken words are turned into text on your device or by a transcription
service and only the text is kept. Your data is stored against your participant code
only.

**Two different things happen to your conversation, and the consent form asks about
them separately.** The app *saves* it so you can look back at it later — that is
how the app works, and it is stored against an anonymous account identifier, not
your name. Separately, we ask whether **the researcher may read it**, which is what
lets us check the app answered correctly and safely. You can say no to the second
and still take part; we would then use only your timing and questionnaire answers.

**Where your words go.** To answer you, the app sends your question to a language
model run by OpenAI, and to speak the answer it sends the text to ElevenLabs. Both
are in the United States. Neither is given your name or any contact details, and
the project's account is not on any plan that uses what you type to train their
models.

Data is stored securely on servers accessible only to the researcher and the
supervisor, kept for six years as required by University policy, and then destroyed.
Anonymised findings will appear in my final project report and may be presented at a
university conference day or published. **Nothing that could identify you will ever
be published.**

## Can I change my mind?

Yes. You can stop at any time without giving a reason and without any disadvantage.

Because your data is stored against a code and not your name, you can ask us to
delete it **up to two weeks after your session** by emailing the researcher and
quoting your participant code. After that we will not be able to identify which data
is yours.
